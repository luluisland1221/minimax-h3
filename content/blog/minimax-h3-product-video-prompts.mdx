> **Quick answer:** A strong MiniMax H3 product video prompt locks product geometry first, then describes one action sequence, one camera path, material behavior, lighting, sound, and exclusions. Start with a four- or five-second 768P test, use a clean product reference when identity matters, and move to 2K only after shape, label, and reflections remain stable.

Product advertising is a strong MiniMax H3 use case because the model combines text direction with image, video, and audio context. A product image can establish geometry and branding, a video can guide camera movement, and audio can guide the sound character. The challenge is that commercial shots are less forgiving than general cinematic scenes: a beautiful clip is unusable when a bottle changes shape, a logo mutates, or a phone gains a second camera.

## Choose the generation mode

Use **text to video** for fictional products, mood studies, packaging concepts, and early art direction. Use **first frame** when the approved product render must be the opening composition. Use **first and last frame** when the shot must land on a controlled end card or final arrangement. Use **multimodal reference** when identity, camera, movement, and sound come from different authorized assets.

Do not upload redundant product images from conflicting angles unless the prompt explains their purpose. Begin with one clean hero image on a simple background. Add a material or movement reference only after the product remains stable.

## Product prompt structure

A reliable commercial brief contains:

1. **Deliverable:** product film, social ad, e-commerce loop, launch teaser, or end card.
2. **Product lock:** shape, color, label, buttons, proportions, and materials that must not change.
3. **Timed action:** what happens first, next, and last.
4. **Camera:** shot size, lens feeling, movement, focus, and whether cuts are allowed.
5. **Light and material:** reflection width, highlight behavior, surface texture, background, and palette.
6. **Sound:** physical effects, ambience, music, and timing.
7. **Exclusions:** extra objects, text changes, deformation, duplicated parts, hands, or watermarks.

## Prompt 3: food and beverage social ad

> Six-second sparkling citrus drink advertisement in a bright studio kitchen. A clear unbranded can with one coral stripe stands on a pale stone counter. Preserve the can height, rim, stripe width, and opening tab. Begin close as cold condensation forms. At two seconds, a citrus slice drops beside the can and releases a small splash. At four seconds, push in as the tab opens once. Crisp daylight, realistic liquid, coral and cream palette. Add tab click, fizz, splash, and quiet kitchen ambience. No hands, no extra cans, no text, no impossible liquid motion.

Fast social ads need one memorable action. Asking for a drop, pour, hand interaction, camera orbit, exploding fruit, logo animation, and end card in six seconds creates competing priorities.

## Settings and credit planning

Use 4–5 seconds at 768P for geometry tests. On minimaxh3.pro, 768P costs 25 credits per generated second and 2K costs 40 before reference adjustments. A five-second base test uses 125 credits at 768P or 200 at 2K. Test at least three prompt versions before committing to a high-resolution pass.

For vertical social delivery choose 9:16. Use 16:9 for web hero films and 1:1 for marketplace tiles. The product should occupy enough of the frame to evaluate shape and label stability.

## Product-video quality checklist

- Does the silhouette match from first frame to last?
- Are buttons, caps, laces, handles, and ports preserved?
- Does label placement remain stable even if text is intentionally blank?
- Do reflections follow the surface instead of floating?
- Are hands anatomically correct when hands are required?
- Does liquid obey gravity and container boundaries?
- Does the camera perform the requested move only once?
- Are sound effects synchronized with visible contact?
- Is the final frame usable for editing or an end card?

Reject a clip when a structural error would require frame-by-frame repair. A slightly different background highlight may be acceptable; a changed product is not.

## How to iterate efficiently

Change one layer at a time. If geometry fails, simplify action and camera before changing the visual style. If reflections flicker, reduce competing light sources. If the label changes, use a clean reference, shorten the shot, keep the surface more frontal, or plan to composite exact typography in post. If sound is wrong, name the physical source and remove unnecessary music.

## FAQ

### Can MiniMax H3 preserve a real product?

An authorized product reference and precise geometry constraints improve the chance, but every output still requires frame-by-frame review.

### Should product videos be generated directly in 2K?

Usually test at 768P first. Generate 2K after the product, action, camera, and sound are stable.

### Can MiniMax H3 render exact brand text?

Text rendering may work, but critical logos, legal copy, and prices should be verified and often composited during editing.

### Which mode is best for e-commerce video?

Use first-frame or multimodal reference when an approved product image must control identity. Use text-to-video for fictional concepts and early ideation.
