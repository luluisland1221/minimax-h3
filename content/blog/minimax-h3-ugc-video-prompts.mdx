> **Quick answer:** MiniMax H3 UGC prompts should define the speaker, product, platform format, first-second hook, natural action, exact dialogue, phone-camera behavior, room sound, and prohibited changes. Use 9:16, keep one speaker and one main action in the first test, and use an authorized identity reference when the same creator must appear across variations.

UGC-style advertising looks casual, but generating it reliably requires precise direction. A vague request for “a realistic influencer video” may produce polished footage that feels like a commercial, offscreen dialogue, changing products, or an overactive camera. The goal is controlled imperfection: a believable phone camera, natural performance, readable action, and audio that does not sound staged.

## What makes generated UGC believable?

Useful UGC usually has five signals:

- A hook that is understandable in the first one or two seconds.
- A visible speaker or a clearly intentional offscreen camera operator.
- A product interaction that can be followed without rapid cuts.
- Conversational dialogue with realistic pauses and emphasis.
- A location with specific but restrained background detail and room tone.

Do not equate shakiness with authenticity. Excessive motion makes faces, hands, and product labels harder to preserve. Ask for subtle handheld drift rather than random vibration.

## Choose the correct H3 mode

Use text-to-video for fictional creators, concept testing, and generic lifestyle scenes. Use first-frame mode when the opening scroll-stopping image is already designed. Use multimodal reference when an authorized creator image controls identity, a separate clip controls gesture or camera rhythm, and audio controls voice character.

For recurring campaigns, keep a continuity sheet with face description, hair, wardrobe, room, device, product, speaking pace, and audio character. The [character consistency guide](/blog/minimax-h3-character-consistency) explains multi-shot planning.

## Prompt 3: lifestyle demonstration

> Nine-second 9:16 outdoor UGC clip at a neighborhood park just after sunrise. A runner in a coral jacket clips a small unbranded safety light to her waist, taps it once, then begins jogging toward a tree-lined path. Start with a close phone-camera view of the clip attaching securely. At three seconds, tilt up to her face as she says: Early miles feel better when I know I can be seen. At six seconds, step backward as she starts moving. Preserve her face, jacket, light shape, clip position, and path direction. Natural breath, fabric movement, birds, distant bicycles, no music, no extra people crossing foreground.

Use a video reference only when a specific gesture or phone-camera rhythm is essential. State that the reference supplies motion, not identity, clothes, or location.

## Dialogue and lip-sync rules

Identify the speaker before the line and keep visible speech within the available time. Use one speaker in the first test. Write the exact words and describe delivery: quiet, amused, skeptical, energetic, or breathless. Avoid asking for dialogue, voice-over, background conversation, music, and several sound effects at once.

If dialogue is spoken offscreen unexpectedly, state that the visible person looks into lens and speaks the line with clearly visible mouth movement. If the camera operator should speak, say that the voice is offscreen and must not move the subject's lips.

## Vertical composition

Use 9:16 for TikTok, Reels, and Shorts concepts. Keep important faces, hands, and products away from the extreme top and bottom where platform controls or captions may cover them. Leave deliberate negative space for real captions added in post. Do not depend on generated captions for legal or promotional copy.

## Credits and iteration

Start with four or five seconds at 768P when testing identity, framing, and one gesture. A five-second 768P base generation uses 125 credits on minimaxh3.pro before reference adjustments. Increase duration only after dialogue pacing works. Use 2K for final candidates that need cropping or detail.

Generate several controlled variations by changing one element:

1. Keep speaker and line; change only the hook framing.
2. Keep framing; change only the product action.
3. Keep action; change only dialogue delivery.
4. Keep the approved performance; change background only if required.

This produces a useful creative matrix instead of eight unrelated clips.

## UGC review checklist

- Is the first-second hook visible and understandable?
- Does the same person remain on screen?
- Are hands and product contact physically plausible?
- Is the dialogue exact and assigned to the correct speaker?
- Does the performance feel conversational rather than theatrical?
- Is handheld motion restrained and intentional?
- Is the background stable enough for the viewer to focus?
- Is there safe space for edited captions?
- Are all claims, endorsements, and disclosures legally appropriate?

Generated characters are not real customers. Do not present fictional testimonials as genuine consumer endorsements. Label synthetic content where law or platform policy requires it.

## FAQ

### Can MiniMax H3 make vertical UGC videos?

Yes. Choose 9:16 and write the scene for phone framing, caption-safe space, and a short social hook.

### How long should UGC dialogue be?

Use one short natural sentence for the first test. Read it aloud within the selected duration before generating.

### Can I use a real creator as a reference?

Only use identity, voice, and performance references you are authorized to process. Obtain consent appropriate to the campaign and jurisdiction.

### Should captions be generated inside the video?

Critical captions, prices, and disclosures are safer to add in editing so typography remains exact and accessible.
